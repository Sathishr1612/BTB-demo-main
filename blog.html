How to Overcome the Fear of Speaking English: Meera’s 60-Day Story

6 min read BTB Academy

Meera had been an accountant for six years. She read English reports easily and wrote flawless emails. But every Monday meeting, when her manager asked, “Meera, any thoughts?”, her mind went blank, her heart raced and she said, “No, sir, all good.”

If this sounds familiar, you are not alone. Fear of speaking English is one of the most common barriers for learners in India, and it has very little to do with intelligence. It is a habit, and habits can change.

Why we freeze when we speak English

Most of us learn English through reading and writing, where we can pause and correct. Speaking is live. The brain worries about grammar, accent and judgement all at once, so it chooses silence. The fix is not “more grammar”. The fix is more safe speaking practice.

“I did not need to learn more English. I needed to hear myself speak it without being afraid.”

What Meera changed in 60 days

  • She spoke for 5 minutes every morning. Alone, to the mirror, about her plans for the day.
  • She stopped translating. She started thinking in short, simple English sentences.
  • She joined a small live class. Role-plays with kind people made mistakes feel normal.
  • She celebrated small wins. One sentence in a meeting was a victory.

By the end of two months, Meera shared a full project update in English, without notes. Nobody noticed her accent. They noticed her ideas.

Try this today

Record a 60-second voice note on “What I did yesterday”. Listen once. Do not judge, just notice. Do it again tomorrow. In a week, you will hear the difference.

5 Daily English Speaking Habits That Work: Arjun’s 30-Day Challenge

5 min read BTB Academy

Arjun was a college student in a small town. He wanted to improve his spoken English, but classes were expensive and his friends spoke only their mother tongue. “Who will I practise with?” he asked.

His answer: himself. He built five tiny habits that took less than 30 minutes a day in total.

1. Think out loud in English

While brushing his teeth or walking to college, Arjun described what he was doing: “I am walking to college. The weather is cloudy.” Simple, but it trained his brain to produce English instantly.

2. Learn five phrases, not fifty words

Single words are hard to use. Phrases such as “I am looking forward to it” or “Could you please explain that again?” are ready to speak. He learned five new phrases a day and used each one at least once.

3. Shadow a speaker

He played a short clip of a speaker he liked and repeated each sentence immediately after, copying the rhythm and pronunciation. This is called shadowing, and it is one of the fastest ways to improve fluency.

4. Keep a “mistake notebook”

Every mistake went into a notebook with the correct sentence beside it. Mistakes became lessons, not embarrassments.

5. Speak with a partner or a trainer weekly

Once a week, he joined a live online class to practise real conversations and get feedback. Feedback is what turns practice into progress.

“Thirty days later, I could introduce myself for two full minutes without stopping.”

Consistency beats intensity. Start with one habit this week and add another next week.

How to Speak Confidently in a Job Interview: Divya’s Offer-Letter Story

7 min read BTB Academy

Divya had a degree, good marks and real project experience. Yet after four interviews, she had no offer. The feedback was always the same: “Good profile, but communication needs to improve.”

So she stopped memorising answers and started learning how to structure them. That is what changed her results.

The simple three-part answer

For almost any interview question, Divya used this pattern:

  • Point: answer in one clear sentence.
  • Proof: give one short example from your life or work.
  • Positive: end with what you learned or achieved.

For “Tell me about yourself”, she moved from a nervous five-minute story to a confident 60-second introduction: who she is, what she has done, and what she wants next.

Three phrases that sound professional

  • “Let me give you an example.”
  • “What I learned from that experience was…”
  • “I would be glad to take that responsibility.”

Practise like it is the real thing

Divya practised mock interviews with a trainer, recorded herself and watched the video. She noticed she spoke too fast and looked down. Small fixes: slow down, pause, smile, look at the camera.

“The interviewer did not hire a perfect speaker. They hired a clear, calm, confident one.”

Three weeks later, Divya received her offer letter. Her skills had not changed. Her communication had.
